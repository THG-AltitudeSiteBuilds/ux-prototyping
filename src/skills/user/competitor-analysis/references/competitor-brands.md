# Competitor Brand Context

General market positioning for the competitors most commonly reviewed against Myprotein in
this repo's reports. This is public brand knowledge, not proprietary research — use it to
calibrate *what lens to bring* to each brand's page, not as a source of specific UI facts
(always verify actual page behaviour live).

If a competitor isn't listed here, use judgement: identify their apparent market position
(value/mass-market, premium/wellness, heritage/performance, etc.) from what you observe on
the page itself, and note in the report which assumption you're working from.

---

## Huel

**Positioning:** Meal-replacement / "nutritionally complete food" brand — broader lifestyle
and convenience audience than traditional sports nutrition, not gym-culture-first. Minimal,
confident brand aesthetic; subscription-first commercial model.

**Lens to apply:** Huel is strong on making a repeat/subscription purchase feel effortless,
and on translating nutrition science into simple, confident claims for a non-expert audience.
Watch for: subscription UX, simplicity of the range (fewer, clearer choices vs SKU sprawl),
and how they build trust around "complete nutrition" claims without overwhelming detail.

---

## The Protein Works (TPW)

**Positioning:** UK sports nutrition brand, closest direct competitor to Myprotein in
audience and model — value/mass-market, huge SKU and flavour range, promotion-heavy,
flavour and taste as a key differentiator.

**Lens to apply:** Because TPW competes on near-identical terms to Myprotein (price, range,
promotions), this is the most likely source of directly transferable patterns. Watch for how
they handle the same structural challenges Myprotein has — large flavour/size matrices,
frequent discounting, bundle/multibuy mechanics — and whether they solve them more clearly.

---

## Optimum Nutrition (ON)

**Positioning:** Global heritage sports nutrition brand, most closely associated with
"Gold Standard Whey" — skews toward serious training credibility and long-standing trust
in gym culture. Sold both DTC and through third-party retailers, which shapes how much their
own site needs to do to build trust versus lean on brand recognition.

**Lens to apply:** ON's advantage is largely brand equity, not necessarily UX innovation —
so opportunities here are more likely to be about how they communicate credibility, product
heritage, and third-party trust signals (retailer availability, athlete endorsements) than
about novel interaction patterns.

---

## Healf

**Positioning:** Premium, editorial-led wellness and biohacking brand — broader "holistic
health" range beyond sports nutrition (supplements, wellness tech, longevity), higher price
point, aspirational content-first aesthetic.

**Lens to apply:** Healf is a useful reference for content and discovery patterns — how they
use editorial content, curation, and "shop the story" mechanics to sell higher-consideration
or unfamiliar products. Less useful as a direct comparison on price-led conversion mechanics,
since their audience and price sensitivity differ from Myprotein's core buyer.

---

## Bulk (Bulk Powders)

**Positioning:** UK value/mass-market sports nutrition brand, large range, aggressive
pricing and promotions — very similar commercial model to Myprotein and TPW.

**Lens to apply:** Like TPW, a close structural competitor — good source for directly
transferable patterns around promotions, bundles, and large-catalogue navigation. Watch for
how they handle promotional density (how many offers can be shown before it becomes noise)
since this is a shared challenge with Myprotein's own promo-heavy pages.

---

## Naked Nutrition

**Positioning:** "Clean"/minimal-ingredient, transparency-led brand — short ingredient
lists, no artificial additives as a core marketing claim. Appeals to a more health-conscious,
ingredient-scrutinising segment; generally a smaller, more premium-niche range than the
mass-market players.

**Lens to apply:** Strongest reference for ingredient transparency and trust-building around
"what's actually in this" — label callouts, comparison to "typical" products, simplified
nutritional communication. Less relevant for range/navigation patterns given their smaller
catalogue.

---

## Gymshark

**Positioning:** Activewear/apparel-first brand (not primarily a nutrition brand) — DTC-only,
built on social and influencer-led marketing with a strong community/brand-culture identity.
Premium athleisure positioning relative to mass-market sportswear.

**Lens to apply:** Most relevant when reviewing Myprotein's **clothing** pages rather than
nutrition pages. Watch for product presentation (fit/sizing confidence, styling context),
community/social proof integration, and how they make apparel feel aspirational rather than
purely functional — areas where a nutrition-first brand's clothing range often lags a
clothing-first competitor.
